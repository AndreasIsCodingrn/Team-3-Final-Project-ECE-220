# Project Proposal Plan (TEAM 3\)

# **Project Abstract**

Reliable and affordable electricity is important for every community, especially in rural areas where power outages can affect homes, businesses, and essential services.

This project will investigate electrical reliability, household energy affordability, and community resilience in Cuba, New Mexico. The study will compare utility-level outage frequency and duration with comparable electric utilities in New Mexico. It will also examine electricity prices and estimated household energy burden using utility rates, household income estimates, and federal energy/affordability datasets. The research will assess potential consequences of interruptions for residents, local businesses, emergency services, and the electrically powered water infrastructure, while differentiating documented local impacts from possible risks. Using Python, Github, and Streamlit, the team will clean and process the data into a dashboard showing historical reliability trends, geographic comparisons, affordability metrics, and possible resilience strategies. The final analysis will evaluate backup generators, solar and associated storage systems, critical-load backup systems, and infrastructure improvements. We will investigate local, state, and federal programs that could help improve energy reliability and affordability as well. Our expected outcomes include a reproducible dataset, a functional dashboard, and credible evidence-based recommendations. Because public outage data are generally reported at the utility rather than village level, the project will explicitly state geographic limitations and seek Cuba-specific records where available/accessible. Our main goal is to use data to identify potential problems and provide practical recommendations that could benefit the community.
